The Scottie Effect

Do players falter on Sunday when Scottie Scheffler is in the hunt?

May 3, 2026

Scottie Scheffler went into the final round of the Cadillac Championship tied for second, six shots behind Cameron Young. Six shots is a big lead, but we've seen enough Scheffler comebacks and opponent collapses to know not to count him out. So I wanted to find out whether opponents really do falter when Scheffler is in the hunt.

I used golfastr, Ray Carpenter's R package that pulls tournament data from ESPN's golf API, with every final round since the start of the 2022 PGA Tour season. That's when Scheffler reached No. 1 in the Official World Golf Ranking. A round counts as Scheffler being in the hunt when:

  • Scheffler is in the top 15, ties included, going into the final round
  • the other player is tied with or ahead of him going into the final round
  • both are within six shots of the leader

I then compared how players scored in those rounds against final rounds without Scheffler chasing them.

The effect is real. In the 284 final rounds that fit, players averaged 0.89 under par. In the other 1,195 they averaged 1.53 under. With Scheffler behind them, players scored 0.64 strokes worse (p = 0.0027).

That Sunday

Young didn't fold. He shot 68 and won by six, wire to wire. Scheffler also shot 68 and finished second.
